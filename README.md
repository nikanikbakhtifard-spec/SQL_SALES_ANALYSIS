SQL Sales Analysis

In this project, I analyzed customer purchasing behavior using PostgreSQL to better understand lifetime value (LTV), revenue trends, and churn patterns in an e-commerce dataset.

The goal was to identify where revenue truly comes from, how customer quality changes over time, and what risks may impact long-term growth.

⸻

Customer Segmentation

I segmented customers based on their total Lifetime Value (LTV) to understand revenue concentration.

Key Findings:
 • The top 25% of customers generate 66% of total revenue ($135.4M).
 • The middle 50% contribute 32% ($66.6M).
 • The bottom 25% account for just 2% ($4.3M).

Revenue is heavily concentrated in a small portion of customers, making retention of high-value users critical.



Cohort Analysis

Customers were grouped by their first purchase year to evaluate how different acquisition cohorts perform over time.

Key Insights:
 • Revenue per customer declines in newer cohorts (2022–2024).
 • Overall revenue growth is driven more by acquiring more customers than increasing customer value.
 • Recent cohorts appear less valuable compared to earlier ones.



Retention & Churn

Churn was defined as no purchase within a 6-month period.

Key Findings:
 • Long-term retention stabilizes at around 8–10%.
 • Churn reaches approximately 90% within 2–3 years.
 • Retention challenges are consistent across cohorts, suggesting a systemic issue rather than a one-time fluctuation.



Strategic Recommendations
 • Protect high-value customers through loyalty or VIP programs.
 • Create upsell opportunities for mid-value customers.
 • Improve early-stage customer engagement to reduce long-term churn.
